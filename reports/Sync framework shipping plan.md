# Ship a thin core, thesis the folder

*As of 2026-11-04. Tags: [F] = backed by the cited source; [M] = inference or unverified model knowledge. Builds on `reports/Local first sync engine research.md` and `reports/Sync engine thesis novelty check.md`; where those disagree with the shipping-plan notes, the shipping-plan notes win.*

Two months is enough to ship `clip` and `time` on a **narrow, app-specific sync core**. It is not enough to ship both products on every surface with payments, push, offline telemetry and E2EE. The core you need is small. One Rust crate holds the SQLite outbox, cursor, apply step, HLC/LWW and the content-addressed blob store, roughly 2–3k lines [M]. One Go connect-rpc service holds a per-scope sequencer row, a mutation table and pull by cursor, roughly 1.5k lines [M]. tusd, APNs/FCM, an OTel collector, Sentry and RevenueCat cover the rest. No buyable sync product fits better: PowerSync would own the ordered log the thesis needs, SQLite Sync needs a paid commercial licence, Turso's offline sync warns of possible data loss, and Electric Cloud is shutting down after the Databricks acquisition. The schedule is set by calendar gates, not code. Google Play makes new personal accounts run a **closed test with 12 testers for 14 consecutive days**, then a production review of up to 7 days. iOS builds need Xcode on a Mac [M]. December finals fall in weeks 5–6. Something has to give, and the honest cut is this: ship both products on desktop, the extension and the CLI, ship `time` on iOS and Android through Tauri, and ship `clip` on Android only. Move `clip` iOS, E2EE, widgets, keyboards, Google Calendar and folder mounts to v1.1 or later. For the thesis, take the novelty from the **filesystem-as-replica laws F1–F5**, which are desktop-only and off the shipping critical path. Pair them with a **load and benchmark chapter on the shared server core (D1)**, because launch needs that load testing anyway. Blob integrity (S1/S2/S5) becomes an oracle, not a headline, since Jazz 2.0 pre-empted most of it.

## Five things are built; everything else is borrowed

Every production engine checked took years, usually with a team. PowerSync's engine dates from 2009 inside JourneyApps ([PowerSync](https://www.powersync.com/company)) [F]. Electric threw away its first architecture in a July 2024 "clean rebuild" ([Electric](https://electric-sql.com/blog/2024/07/17/electric-next)) [F] and needed a new storage engine in v1.1 ([Electric v1.1](https://electric-sql.com/blog/2025/08/13/electricsql-v1.1-released)) [F]. Linear's CTO had built sync engines for a decade before Linear ([localfirst.fm](https://chartable.com/podcasts/localfirstfm/episodes/178924192-15-tuomas-artman-linear-sync-engines-rethought-startup-mvp)) [F]. The solo precedent is the warning. Actual Budget's LWW-over-HLC sync worked, but James Long was "lonely and burnt-out", could not continue solo, and open-sourced the app ([tmpdir](https://community.tmpdir.org/t/actual-budget-is-switching-to-open-source/497)) [F]. The sync algorithm did not end that project. Maintaining the whole product alone did. Rewrites in these projects came from generality: many backends, many write patterns, partial replication. An engine for two known schemas and one owner per scope avoids most of that [M]. So the rule is to own only what the thesis and the invariants need, and borrow everything else.

The buy options fail for specific reasons. **PowerSync Open Edition** is the only credible mobile row-sync product. Its service is FSL (commercial use allowed apart from competing with PowerSync, converting to open source after two years) and its client SDKs are Apache-2.0 ([PowerSync licensing](https://powersync.com/legal/overview)) [F]. Its operation IDs are instance-local, though, and it owns the ordered log the thesis's per-scope sequencer and hash chain must control, so adopting it would split the thesis engine from the product engine [M]. **SQLite Sync** is Elastic-2.0 and needs a commercial licence for commercial production use ([pub.dev licence](https://pub.dev/packages/sqlite_sync/versions/1.1.1/license)) [F]. **Turso offline sync** was a beta with "no durability guarantees… data loss is possible" ([Turso](https://turso.tech/blog/turso-offline-sync-public-beta)) [F]. **Electric** was acquired by Databricks on 2026-08-11, and Electric Cloud is shutting down ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/databricks-electric-wasm-agentic-postgres/)) [F]. LiveStore was still pre-1.0 with breaking changes ([changelog](https://docs.livestore.dev/changelog)) [F]. Vendor churn is itself an argument for a thin core on SQLite and Postgres, which carries no vendor-lifetime risk.

\* `fs_entry` and the mount plugin sit behind a Cargo feature and are left out of mobile builds.

The design invariant is the one from the novelty check. **The scope's version counter, the mutation's effects and the idempotency record commit in one Postgres transaction.** Everything else is downstream and may be stale, duplicate messages or drop them, because clients recover by pulling by cursor. That is how Zero, PowerSync, Electric, Figma, Notion and Linear are built: one ordered source per scope, disposable readers ([Zero](https://zero.rocicorp.dev/docs/self-host); [Linear](https://linear.app/now/scaling-the-linear-sync-engine)) [F]. Take `server_version` from `UPDATE sync_scope SET head = head + 1 … RETURNING` inside the mutation transaction. Never use `bigserial`, Redis `INCR` or a Kafka offset, because sequences commit out of order and cursor pulls then skip rows. Advance `last_mutation_id` in the same transaction even when a mutation is rejected ([Replicache](https://doc.replicache.dev/reference/server-push)) [F]. Return rejections as 2xx with a status, because non-2xx responses block client upload queues ([PowerSync](https://docs.powersync.com/handling-writes/handling-write-validation-errors)) [F]. Give each command a `uuid` plus a `temp_id` mapping in Todoist's style ([Todoist](https://developer.todoist.com/api/v1/)) [F]. Put `protocol_version` in push and pull from day one, so that E2EE and the hash chain arrive later as a migration rather than a rewrite [M].

Two clients cannot host the Rust core cheaply. In v1 the **Chrome MV3 extension** should be an online-only client of the server API. Compiling the core to WASM can wait [M]. The **Go CLI** should talk to the local `clip` daemon over a Unix socket when it is running, and to the server API otherwise. Don't reimplement the core in Go [M].

## Mobile is mostly OS extensions, and iOS sets the hardest limits

### Shells: Tauri for `time`, thin native for `clip`

`time` already has a Rust and React codebase. Tauri 2 mobile reuses it, and the official notification, sql, store, deep-link, biometric, clipboard and upload plugins all cover iOS and Android ([plugins-workspace](https://github.com/tauri-apps/plugins-workspace)) [F]. The risk is maturity. The Tauri team said 2.0 was not the "mobile as a first class citizen" release, and that not all plugins are ported ([Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/blog/tauri-2-0-0-release-candidate/)) [F]. One team's May 2026 evaluation rejected it as "closer to alpha" ([range-day-docs](https://range-day-docs.pages.dev/decisions/Tauri-vs-Capacitor)) [F, single source]. Widgets, share extensions and background refresh need hand-written Swift and Kotlin targets anyway ([tauri-plugin-widgets](https://docs.rs/crate/tauri-plugin-widgets/0.3.0/source/README.md)) [F]. `clip`'s valuable mobile surfaces are exactly those OS extensions (share sheet, keyboard, Quick Settings tile), so a Tauri webview adds little. Thin SwiftUI and Compose apps over UniFFI are the lower-risk choice. One practitioner reports wrappers of 207 lines of Kotlin and 113 of Swift over 6,740 lines of Rust ([levelup](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/the-headless-mobile-architecture-bypassing-the-kmp-internal-war-with-rust-99d759e02f50)) [F, anecdote]. Kotlin Multiplatform is a poor fit, because it would mean a second implementation of the core. Mozilla moved away from exactly that ([Firefox docs](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/_sources/rust-components/developing-rust-components/index.md.txt)) [F], and PowerSync moved its Swift SDK off KMP to native Swift in mid-2026 ([PowerSync](https://releases.powersync.com/announcements/powersync-kotlin-sdk)) [F]. If Tauri mobile fails the week-1 spike, the fallback for `time` is React Native/Expo with uniffi-bindgen-react-native over the same crate ([Mozilla Hacks](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2024/12/introducing-uniffi-for-react-native-rust-powered-turbo-modules/)) [F]. Packaging UniFFI for four or more targets (XCFramework, AAR via cargo-ndk) typically takes 1–2 weeks to stabilize in CI [M]. Start it in week 1.

### Hard platform constraints

**Clipboard capture.** Neither OS lets an app monitor the clipboard in the background. Since iOS 16, a programmatic read of `UIPasteboard.general` triggers a prompt. `UIPasteControl` pastes without the prompt ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uipastecontrol)) [F], and `changeCount` can be read without permission, which is how Firefox iOS decides to offer paste ([firefox-ios](https://www.github.com/mozilla-mobile/firefox-ios/pull/20200)) [F]. Paste, the market leader, states that it cannot read the clipboard in the background. It captures only when the user opens the app, uses the share extension or copies through its keyboard ([Paste help](https://pasteapp.io/help/clipboard-history-on-iphone)) [F]. On Android 10+, only the default IME or the focused app can read the clipboard ([Android](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/risks/secure-clipboard-handling)) [F]. KDE Connect's workaround is a notification action that foregrounds an activity briefly ([KDE MR 145](https://invent.kde.org/network/kdeconnect-android/-/merge_requests/145)) [F]. So mobile `clip` v1 offers explicit capture: an in-app UIPasteControl button, a share extension and an App Intent on iOS; a Quick Settings tile or notification action that opens a transparent activity, plus a share target, on Android. Paste-out works by tapping a synced item. Mark sensitive clips with `EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE`. Tell users plainly that "copy on phone, appears on desktop" needs a tap. Apple's Universal Clipboard has no third-party API [M].

**Background execution.** Neither OS gives a sync engine reliable periodic background time. BGTaskScheduler gives about 30 s every 15+ minutes, best-effort ([background-service plugin](https://docs.rs/crate/tauri-plugin-background-service/0.5.2)) [F]. Apple says not to send more than "two or three" background pushes per hour, and drops held pushes after a force-quit ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)) [F]. On Android 15+, `dataSync` foreground services are capped at 6 h per 24 h, and the app crashes if it does not stop on timeout ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fg-service-timeout)) [F]. Make `sync_once()` bounded and resumable inside a 25 s slice. Sync on launch and foreground, and after local mutations (debounced). Use push only as a nudge. Hand blob transfers to the OS: background `URLSession` on iOS with file-based, chunked presigned parts, reconciled on relaunch; WorkManager chains on Android, with user-initiated data transfer jobs for large uploads ([Android UIDT](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt)) [F]. Background URLSession tasks also die on force-quit ([Apple DTS](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765196)) [F].

**The App Group DB pitfall.** Do not put the live SQLite database in an iOS App Group container shared with extensions. iOS kills suspended processes that hold a file lock (0xdead10cc). Marco Arment, Ryan Ashcraft and GRDB's author report persistent crashes with no clean mitigation ([mjtsai roundup](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/05/15/sqlite-databases-in-app-group-containers-dont/); [GRDB](https://swiftpackageindex.com/groue/GRDB.swift/v7.5.0/documentation/grdb/databasesharing)) [F]. Keep the database in the app's private container. Extensions write one append-only "inbox" file per capture into the App Group, and the main app imports each one as a `capture_clip` mutation. Widgets and the keyboard read a JSON snapshot the app writes. Use `CompleteUntilFirstUserAuthentication` file protection so background sync works while the phone is locked [M]. On Android, extensions run in the same process and UID, so the database can be shared directly [M].

**Folder mounts are desktop-only.** iOS cannot watch user-picked folders in the background, and a two-way mount there means building a File Provider extension, a project of several weeks [M]. On Android, FileObserver does not work on SAF tree URIs, and `MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` is restricted by Play [M]. The mobile replica set is therefore {SQLite, CAS}. Keep `fs_entry` and the codecs behind a Cargo feature. This is also why the thesis's lead claim stays off the mobile critical path.

**Key storage (v1.1, but design it now).** Generate a random 256-bit account key and keep it in three wrapped forms. On the device: a Keychain item with `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly`, or an Android Keystore wrap [M]. For recovery: a recovery code through Argon2id. Optionally: a passkey PRF wrap. PRF works on iOS 18+ and Android API 28+, does not work on simulators, must be enabled when the passkey is created, and matches across devices only if the passkey syncs ([maktub_passkey](https://pub.dev/documentation/maktub_passkey/latest/)) [F]. Run the PRF output through HKDF to get the wrapping key ([Tinfoil](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/guides/passkey-kit)) [F]. Expose a `KeyProvider` callback in UniFFI so the Rust core never calls keystore APIs directly. Do not make biometrics the key-access policy, because a biometric-bound key blocks background sync [M].

**Store rules.** iOS rejects apps that omit privacy manifests for required-reason APIs (ITMS-91053). UserDefaults is one such API, and SQLite wrappers commonly trigger the file-timestamp and disk-space declarations ([Nutrient](https://www.nutrient.io/blog/privacy-manifests-on-ios/)) [F]. Since 31 Aug 2026, Play requires new apps and updates to **target API 36** ([Android](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)) [F]. The Google Play closed-test gate is described in the plan section. Both stores require in-app account deletion. Request only background fetch and remote-notification modes, and declare FGS types honestly [M]. In the extension, the `"notifications"` permission must ship in the **first** sync-capable release, because adding it later disables existing installs until users re-approve ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/how-to/integrate/web-push)) [F].

**Billing.** Use StoreKit 2 and Play Billing through RevenueCat, which is free until $2,500 monthly tracked revenue. That is about 250 subscribers at $10 per month [M]. Use Stripe for desktop. Keep the entitlement check in the sync service, so paid-only sync is refused without one. US storefronts may link out to web checkout after the 2025 Epic v. Apple injunction. Sources disagree on whether any commission remains ([Median](https://median.co/blog/epic-v-apple-payment-links-ruling-explained)) [F]. RevenueCat says the ruling applies only to the US ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/faqs)) [F]. Outside the US you still need IAP [M].

## Push is a poke, Postgres is the queue, and Kafka waits

Every push channel is lossy, coalescing and throttled. APNs may reorder notifications, and stores **only one** pending notification per bundle while a device is offline ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/sending-notification-requests-to-apns)) [F]. FCM downgrades high-priority messages that do not produce visible notifications, judged over 7 days ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority)) [F]. The design follows from that. A push payload carries at most `{scope, head_version}`. Set the collapse ID (`apns-collapse-id`, FCM `collapse_key`) to `scope:{id}` so only the latest poke survives. A client that receives `head ≤ cursor` ignores it. Duplicates, reordering and loss are therefore all harmless, and correctness lives entirely in pull by cursor [M]. For `clip`, this means a backgrounded phone cannot be woken reliably to prefetch clips. Clips arrive on the next open, or through an optional visible "clip from desktop" alert.

**E2EE push (v1.1).** On iOS, a Notification Service Extension runs only for alert pushes with `mutable-content: 1`, and has about 30 s to rewrite the content ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/modifying-content-in-newly-delivered-notifications)) [F]. The entitlement that lets an NSE drop a push silently is meant for encrypted messaging apps, and other use cases report rejection ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/707406)) [F]. So every alert must show *something*. Send generic text ("New reminder"). The NSE decrypts a ciphertext body that fits under 4 KB, or fetches one, and rewrites the alert. If it times out, the generic text leaks nothing [M]. This is the Matrix `event_id_only` pattern ([Matrix spec](https://spec.matrix.org/latest/push-gateway-api/)) [F]. On Android, send FCM **data** messages, never `notification` messages, decrypt in `onMessageReceived`, post locally, and set `proxy: DENY` [M]. Timing, size and device token still leak to Apple and Google. State this in the privacy policy.

**Local reminders for `time`.** Reminders are local notifications that each device schedules from synced data. They work offline, survive server outages and are E2EE-safe. iOS caps an app at **64 pending notification requests**, "a system limit and there is no way around it" ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171)) [F]. Keep a rolling window of about 50, and rebuild it deterministically after every pull batch that touches reminder rows. Give each one a deterministic ID such as `reminder:{task_id}:{occurrence}`, so the diff against pending OS requests is idempotent [M]. On Android, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is not pre-granted to fresh installs targeting 13+, and is revocable. `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is limited to alarm and calendar apps under Play policy. Alarms die on reboot ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule)) [F]. Ask for exact alarms with a rationale screen, fall back to inexact, and re-register on `BOOT_COMPLETED`. Store reminders as `(rule, tz_id, local_wall_time)`, not as UTC instants, so DST changes behave correctly. "Dismiss everywhere" is an ordinary synced `reminder_ack` mutation. Accept that a stale banner may linger on iOS until the next sync [M]. The extension can use `chrome.alarms`. The CLI has no reminders.

**Real-time channel.** Connect in browsers supports **server streaming only** ([Connect](https://connectrpc.com/docs/web/getting-started)) [F]. That is all a poke channel needs. One `Subscribe(scopes, cursors) returns stream Poke` RPC serves native clients over HTTP/2 and the extension over connect-web. Push and pull stay unary. This replaces the "WebSocket gateway" in ARCHITECTURE §9 without loss. Mobile holds the stream only in the foreground. Reconnect uses full-jitter exponential backoff and always pulls before trusting pokes. Electric's Durable Streams (offsets that clients resume from, CDN-cacheable catch-up) is close to this pull protocol and worth borrowing conventions from, but not depending on ([Electric](https://electric.ax/blog/2025/12/09/announcing-durable-streams)) [F].

**Google Calendar (deferred).** Calendar is an external event stream with the same poke-then-pull shape, inverted. Watch notifications have no body, are not guaranteed, and don't auto-renew ([Google](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/push)) [F]. Changes come from `syncToken` incremental pulls, and **410 Gone** forces a full resync ([Google](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/sync)) [F]. A correct mirror needs a watch handler, a coalesced job queue, renewal cron, a fallback poll, echo tagging through `extendedProperties.private`, and OAuth verification for calendar scopes. It also needs a plaintext server, which conflicts with E2EE scopes [M]. That is several weeks of work. Defer it. If one piece must ship, ship a one-way import.

**Minimal backend stack, and the Kafka conflict.** Postgres is the log, the outbox, the job queue (push dispatch, claims, later gcal) and the inbox, drained by `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` workers in the same Go binary. Pokes go through one lossy bus. Redis pub/sub is explicitly at-most-once ([Redis](https://redis.io/docs/latest/develop/pubsub/)) [F], which is fine only because clients recover by pull. Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY delivers only on commit and has no persistence ([PostgreSQL 18](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-notify.html)) [F]. Recall.ai found that a NOTIFY inside a transaction takes a global lock at commit, which serialized commits and caused outages under many writers ([Recall.ai](https://www.recall.ai/blog/postgres-listen-notify-does-not-scale)) [F]. DBOS reports that batching NOTIFYs keeps that lock off the per-write path ([DBOS](https://dbos.dev/blog/postgres-listen-notify-scalability)) [F]. So in v1, send NOTIFY from the **batched outbox relay, never inside mutation transactions**. That adds no new infrastructure. Keep a `PokeBus` interface and switch to NATS core when load tests say so. ARCHITECTURE §9 plans "outbox → Kafka" for February. Resolve the conflict by **keeping §9's outbox schema and version-gated consumers unchanged, and replacing only the sink**: poke bus plus in-process workers now, Kafka as an additional outbox sink once there is a second consumer service that needs replay. Kafka's exactly-once ends at Kafka's boundary anyway. External effects need idempotency keys and an inbox table whatever bus you use ([Kafka docs](https://kafka.apache.org/42/design/design/)) [F]. §9 already argues that reclaim work belongs in a Postgres queue, because "Kafka has no delayed delivery" (local, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §9).

## Telemetry gets its own droppable spool, never the sync log

Telemetry must not ride the sync log. The log is ordered, durable, per-user, eventually E2EE and never dropped. Telemetry is unordered, droppable, aggregated, consent-gated and kept for 14–90 days [M]. Sharing the user-data outbox causes head-of-line blocking of user mutations. It inflates the `outbox.depth` metric the load tests measure, adds write amplification to the hot Postgres path, and ties every event to an account ID. Existing sync products keep diagnostics separate too. PowerSync exposes client `SyncStatus` and recommends shipping sync errors through a separate logger such as Sentry ([PowerSync](https://docs.powersync.com/maintenance-ops/production-readiness-guide)) [F]. Share the *client primitive*, not the server log. One `AppendOnlySpool` type gets two instances. `outbox` is ordered and never dropped. `telemetry.spool` is bounded at about 10 MB, evicts oldest first, and is purged on consent revocation. It is the lowest network priority, paused while the sync outbox is non-empty or the network is metered [M]. For `time`, ActivityWatch events, window titles and app names are **product data**: they sync through the engine, never into telemetry.

Every major SDK buffers on disk with bounds and drops oldest. Sentry keeps 30 envelopes ([sentry-docs](https://github.com/getsentry/sentry-docs/pull/4566.diff)) [F]. PostHog iOS keeps 1,000 events ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/docs/libraries/ios/configuration)) [F]. **Privacy and compliance.** First-party, content-free telemetry is not ATT "tracking", but fingerprinting is prohibited regardless, and you are responsible for every SDK's code ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)) [F]. E2EE data is not "collected" ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)) [F]. The Chrome Web Store's Limited Use policy allows only data needed for the single purpose, including reliability, and requires disclosure inside the extension's UI ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/limited-use/)) [F]. The EDPB's ePrivacy Art. 5(3) guidelines reach beyond cookies to "certain types of local processing where information is transferred" ([EDPB](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)) [F]. Under **PIPA**, the 2023 amendment (in force 2023-09-15) allows overseas storage or entrustment if it is disclosed in the privacy policy, and the PIPC can order a transfer suspended ([BKL](https://www.bkl.co.kr/en/law/insight/newsletter/5374)) [F]. The simplest compliant posture for a privacy-branded product works like this [M]. Crash reports and analytics are opt-in, with a prompt on first crash. Use a random install ID rotated every 30–90 days, never derived from the account. Attributes come from a typed allowlist per product, with no free-form strings except error codes. A CI test fuzzes clip content and window titles through every error path and asserts that none reach the spool. Sentry SaaS and Grafana Cloud are listed as overseas entrustees in the 개인정보처리방침, or you self-host the collector in a Korean region. Vendor claims of "no consent needed" (Aptabase, TelemetryDeck) are vendors' positions, not regulator rulings ([TelemetryDeck](https://telemetrydeck.com/docs/articles/anonymization-how-it-works/)) [F].

## The thesis takes novelty from folders and rigor from load

### Weighing the three candidates

| | (a) Filesystem-as-replica F1–F5 | (b) Distributed sync server D1 | (c) Blob integrity S1/S2/S5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Novelty | **Strongest.** Jazz 2.0 has no filesystem replica, and the file-synchronizer verification line stops at Syncpal (2019) ([Jazz SPEC](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/10_lenses_migrations.md); [Syncpal](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-22496-7_1)) [F]. F4/F5 are unclaimed laws | **Low.** Every rule has a citation; Jazz's sharding chapter is "exploratory" ([Jazz](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/15_sharding.md)) [F], so a *tested* D1 is open but thin | **Narrowed.** Jazz specifies blobs inside the row transaction, base-following rejection cascade and lease-aware GC ([Jazz](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/3_transactions.md)) [F]; only cross-row/cross-user S2 and retention-window S5 survive |

| Load-test story | Novel metrics, custom tooling | **Cleanest**: pgbench, k6, ablations that produce violations | Reuses ARCHITECTURE §14 hotspots |
| Main risk | Logseq ships two-way Markdown with field-level merge ([Logseq](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/whats-new-with-logseq-db-may-16th-2026/35020)) [F] | Reviewers call it engineering | S2's cross-user case needs a third app |

**Recommendation.** Lead with **(a) F1–F5** as the contribution chapter. Add **(b)'s load and benchmark chapter** on the same shared core, framed as "the substrate holds D1 under realistic load", not as a novelty claim. That chapter costs little extra, because B1, B3 and B6 are pre-launch work. Demote **(c)** to an oracle. S1 becomes an invariant checked in every simulation, and S5 an optional section only if January runs ahead. S6, the runtime trust-mode switch the novelty check ranked second, stays a stretch goal tied to whether E2EE ships behind its flag in February. Don't write "first two-way file ↔ DB sync". Write "the first app-agnostic engine where a mounted folder is a full replica peer, with crash recovery (F4) and echo freedom (F5) stated and tested". The precedents to cite are ViewBox for F4 ([FAST'14](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast14/technical-sessions/presentation/zhang)) [F] and "Mysteries of Dropbox" for the method ([ICST'16](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~bcpierce/papers/mysteriesofdropbox.pdf)) [F].

The laws, from the earlier report. **F1** `decode(render(rows)) = rows`. **F2** `render(decode(file)) = file`, so a round trip never rewrites a file the user didn't change. **F3** a concurrent disk edit is never silently overwritten. **F4** after any crash point, the scan restores `fs_entry` consistent with disk and the database, with no lost or duplicated mutations. **F5** a quiescent system performs no further writes. Lightroom's August 2026 bug, detecting conflicts and rewriting XMP when nothing changed, is a live failure of F2 and F5 in a shipping product ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/bug-reports-674/p-lightroom-classic-repeatedly-detects-metadata-conflicts-and-writes-xmp-changes-when-no-edits-were-made-1635848)) [F]. It makes the motivation concrete.

**The fleet simulator is itself a contribution.** None of the public sync benchmarks models offline devices with outboxes and reconnect storms. Electric reports only averages, without hardware or p99 ([Electric](https://electric.ax/docs/reference/benchmarks)) [F]. PowerSync publishes a single-client benchmark, each value the mean of 10 runs ([PowerSync](https://powersync.com/blog/how-fast-is-powersync-performance-benchmarks-for-flutter)) [F]. The Go simulator runs N real client cores in-process. Each device follows a scripted lifecycle: online think-time, offline for a lognormal duration while it accumulates Q mutations, then reconnect. Arrivals are open-loop on a Poisson schedule [M]. On determinism: Go's `testing/synctest` (stable since Go 1.25) gives fake time and quiescence detection but **not** deterministic goroutine interleaving ([Applied Go](https://appliedgo.net/spotlight/go-1.25-the-synctest-package/)) [F]. Seed-reproducible schedules need your own single-threaded event loop at message boundaries, in the style of TigerBeetle's VOPR ([VOPR](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/about/vopr)) [F]. ER-π (Middleware 2025) is the simulator baseline to compare against ([PDF](https://people.cs.vt.edu/tilevich/papers/middleware2025.pdf)) [F].

**Baselines.** The strongest, fairest baselines are ablations of your own system. Each one must *produce* violations: `bigserial` vs scope-row, cache-aside vs log pull, poke loss without periodic pull, Redis-held vs DB-held idempotency keys, watcher without echo suppression, apply without an intent record. Add a naive watcher+LWW strawman on the same stack. Compare Syncthing **only on the local half** (detection, scan, echo, crash safety), because it is peer-to-peer and has no Postgres. Use PowerSync Open Edition as the server-side reference, after checking its licence for a DeWitt clause before publishing numbers [M].

**Measurement rules.** Use the **open model**. A closed loop lets slow responses suppress arrivals, which k6's docs call coordinated omission ([k6](https://grafana.com/docs/k6/latest/using-k6/scenarios/concepts/open-vs-closed/)) [F]. Schroeder et al. show that closed-model response time depends on the multiprogramming level ([NSDI'06](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi-06/open-versus-closed-cautionary-tale)) [F]. Measure latency from the *intended* start time, as pgbench `--rate` does, and report its schedule lag ([pgbench](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/pgbench.html)) [F]. Record into HdrHistogram, and compare corrected with raw histograms as Gil Tene recommends ([mechanical-sympathy](https://groups.google.com/g/mechanical-sympathy/c/icNZJejUHfE)) [F]. In k6, watch `dropped_iterations`: any drop means the target rate was not actually offered. Report p50/p90/p99/p99.9/max with sample counts. p99.9 needs roughly 10^4–10^5 samples per configuration. Never average percentiles across runs; merge histograms instead [M]. Follow Hoefler & Belli SC'15: report variability and distributions, use nonparametric statistics on skewed data, and stop on a stated criterion such as "until the 99% CI is within 5% of the mean" ([Hoefler & Belli](https://evaluate.inf.usi.ch/node/366)) [F]. Run ≥5–10 trials on a fresh database each, discard warmup, and report median plus a bootstrap 95% CI. Compare designs with Mann-Whitney U. Report throughput **at an SLO**, not maximum throughput. Pin the load generator to separate cores or a separate host. Fix CPU frequency, pin `GOMAXPROCS`, record all versions and `postgresql.conf` diffs, and interleave A/B/A/B trials on cloud hardware [M]. Ship the harness, seeds and raw logs, targeting ACM "Artifacts Available + Functional" [M].

## Conclusion

The shipping plan and the thesis turn out to depend on the same small core. The server rules that make the product survive outages, reconnect storms and push loss (one sequencer row per scope, pull by cursor, lossy pokes) are the D1 hypotheses the load chapter measures. The fleet simulator that answers "can we launch?" is the artifact that answers "is this reproducible?". The folder replica is off the mobile critical path, because mobile operating systems forbid what it needs, and the desktop is where it can carry the novelty. The real constraint on two months is not engineering difficulty but serial calendar gates plus one person's hours. Every scope decision should be judged by whether it moves a gate. The Play closed test and the Tauri go/no-go both qualify. Most features do not.

One finding should change how you think about the products. On mobile, a clipboard manager is an *explicit-capture* product: the OS never lets it watch the clipboard. A task manager's reminders must be *local-first*, because push cannot be trusted to deliver them. Both facts push in the same direction. The value of the sync framework is that every device holds a correct local replica and acts on it alone, with the server only ordering and nudging. That is the architecture above, and it is also the most defensible thing to say about it in the thesis.
