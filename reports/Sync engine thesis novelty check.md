# Claim the contract, not the sync engine

No single mechanism in the proposal is new. A server-ordered log of multi-row mutations, content-addressed blobs with leases, lens-law file codecs, per-field merge rules, CRDT fields, and a deterministic validator that clients replay over an encrypted log all have direct prior art. That prior art runs from Bayou (1995) and SPORC (2010) through Harmony, Replicache/Zero, PowerSync, Figma and Linear, up to Encrypted Spaces (June 2026). A November 2026 sweep of about 200 papers, specs and design documents (Appendix A) removed more. The decisive find is the **Jazz 2.0 SPEC** (alpha, edited through November 2026). It already specifies a SQLite relational local-first database with authority-side serializable validation, a rejection cascade to writes built on a rejected write, blobs stored as content-addressed chunk trees committed inside the row transaction, and upload leases, receipts and refcount GC ([Jazz SPEC](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/1_intro.md)) [F]. That pre-empts most of what this report earlier ranked first (S1, S2, S5). The claims now rank as follows. **Strongest: F4/F5**, crash recovery and echo freedom for a folder that acts as a replica of a relational sync engine. Jazz has no filesystem replica, and no file-synchronizer verification paper appeared after 2019. **Second: S6 as a runtime trust-mode switch**, backed by a per-user hash-chained log and distribution transparency (D1). Jazz lists "per-column encryption and authorization" as an open question. Acumen (OSDI 2026) and SignalCD (USENIX Security 2026) secure E2EE collaboration but switch nothing at runtime. **Third, narrowed: S2, S5 and S1.** S2 now covers only cross-row and semantic dependents, dependents seen by other users' devices, and a durable rejection notice. S5 covers only GC under a retention window that honors bases held by long-offline devices. S1 covers only a pending → committed → revoked state that other devices can observe. The integration gap still holds, though more weakly: **no product or paper found combines relational rows, blobs, two-way user folders, optional E2EE and server-side validation (R+B+F+E+V)**. Jazz now reaches R+B+V with E as byte formats only. PowerSync/Convex (R+B+V) and Syncthing/Obsidian Sync/rclone (B+F+E) remain the other two clusters. On impact, demand is proven in vertical, connectivity-hostile domains that combine forms and photos: inspection, field service, healthcare, POS and airlines. The 2025–2026 vendor wave, though, ended in shutdowns and acqui-hires. Realm Device Sync reached end of life. Triplit, Electric Cloud and Instant Cloud are gone or closing. That says standalone sync SDK economics are weak and adopters insist on bring-your-own Postgres and self-hosting. For a four-month thesis, frame it as **"a specified, simulation-checked contract for offline-first relational records that reference files and blobs, mirrored into user folders, under server rejection and a switchable trust mode"**, and cite Jazz 2.0 as the closest system. Demonstrate cross-domain reach with three small apps whose workloads differ: `clip`, `time`, and a forms-plus-photos inspection app. Do not rest it on adoption or on a feature checklist against Ditto or PowerSync. Tags below: **[F]** is verified this session from a fetched or search-surfaced source; **[M]** is model memory, not re-verified, and is a lead to check before you cite it.

### Validator and E2EE: SPORC did it in 2010

The earlier report said "none of the surveyed products has this design". That holds for products. It **does not hold for the literature**, and a thesis that repeats it will be rejected by any reviewer who knows SPORC. In SPORC the server's "sole purpose is to order and store client-generated operations" and payloads are encrypted. SPORC already has the dual role: "a non-malicious server will immediately reject operations from users with insufficient privileges", yet "every client maintains its own copy of the ACL ... and refuses to apply operations that came from unauthorized users" ([SPORC](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall15/cos518/papers/sporc.pdf)) [F]. That is exactly "the server verdict is an early answer, client replay is the authority". SPORC also has a hash-chained history, fork* consistency, and a local-coherence invariant equivalent to S6. Bayou requires every write's dependency check and merge procedure to be deterministic, with no time- or machine-dependent inputs, and replays in primary commit order ([Bayou notes](https://mwhittaker.github.io/papers/html/terry1995managing.html)) [F]. The closest current work is **Encrypted Spaces** (Harvard, June 2026), where "the same apply function is executed in three settings" (server, zkVM prover and client) so that all three "reach the same decision", including app-declared assertions such as "prevent removing a non-empty directory" ([whitepaper](https://encryptedspaces.org/whitepapers/encrypted-spaces.pdf)) [F]. A 2026 open-source design, sens-o-matic, moves all validation into "one deterministic Rust crate" over a ciphertext log that the server orders, and pins the rule version per entity ([issue #61](https://github.com/n8behavior/sens-o-matic/issues/61)) [F].

The gap that remains is the **switch**, not the mechanism. Encrypted Spaces decides plaintext versus encrypted statically, per column, and lists "verifying encrypted column values" as future work. It has no per-user runtime mode change and no migration. Actual Budget offers opt-in E2EE on the same protocol but runs no validator in either mode ([Actual](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/sync/)) [F]. Apple ADP shows that switching on E2EE means losing server features ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/102630)) [F]. The claim that survives: *validation placement is a runtime trust-mode parameter, not an architectural fork. The same validator over the same server order yields identical decisions in both modes (S6), and a defined cut-point migration moves between modes.* Two engineering obligations come with that claim. First, a **per-user hash chain** `h_n = H(h_{n-1} ‖ server_version_n ‖ device_id ‖ H(payload_n))`, so that S6 means "the same hash-identified prefix" rather than "whatever the server chose to show me". Atproto's signed state root, which "almost always" has a null `prev`, shows that a state root alone cannot detect dropped ops ([atproto spec](https://atproto.com/specs/repository)) [F]. Second, a **required self-check**: clients replay in standard mode too, so that drift between native and WASM validator builds surfaces as a test failure. No published measurement of that kind of determinism failure was found.

The 2025–2026 papers crowd the mechanism but leave the switch open. **Acumen** (Berkeley/Stanford, OSDI 2026) gives CRDT editors confidentiality, integrity and **fork-causal consistency**, plus verifiable snapshots for newcomers and a secure GC, at 25 users with negligible added latency ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi26/presentation/cottone)) [F]. **SignalCD** (EPFL/MPI, USENIX Security 2026) proves that an E2EE asynchronous broadcast channel plus *any* reconciliation that yields globally consistent views is secure ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/knabenhans)) [F]. That result supports S6's modularity argument: the validator is orthogonal to the encrypted channel. **Proof-Carrying CRDTs** (KIT, PaPoC 2025) attach validity proofs to updates so replicas check them without replay ([PaPoC 2025](https://papoc-workshop.github.io/2025/program.html)) [F]. That is an alternative to full client replay and belongs in the related-work comparison. MLS already assumes a delivery service that totally orders commits, and its transcript hash binds members to one history ([RFC 9420](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9420)) [F]. The sync server could therefore act as the MLS delivery service. Jazz 2.0 ships E2EE envelope formats, yet its authorization chapter lists "per-column encryption and authorization" as an open design question ([Jazz](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/7_authorization.md)) [F]. None of these has a per-user runtime mode switch, a migration between modes, or a cross-mode equivalence test. Acumen and SignalCD were read only through their abstracts. If either one orders through a server with a plaintext fallback, S6 narrows to the migration alone.

### Blob rejection semantics: Jazz 2.0 closed the easy half

**Correction.** This section was titled "the clearest gap" before the literature sweep. That no longer holds. The Jazz 2.0 SPEC specifies the same-row and blob cases. Rejection "cascades only along bases … writes built on it … are rejected too (`predecessor_rejected`)", while every other pending write "settle[s] by [its] own fate" (INV-TX-8) ([3_transactions](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/3_transactions.md)) [F]. A write rejected locally because its large value was never staged cascades the same way ([4_history_merging](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/4_history_merging.md)) [F]. Large values are chunk trees committed inside the row transaction, and staged chunks stay unreachable from any row until their commit lands ([19_large_values](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/19_large_values.md)) [F]. So a rejected Jazz write cannot leave an orphan blob by construction. Three things remain open. Jazz forbids cross-row dependency edges (INV-HIST-18), and its mergeable transactions carry "no ... causal dependency graph", so a semantic dependent in another row is decided on its own. Jazz relays serve exactly one auth scope (§9.5), so no other user's device ever builds on a pending write. And Jazz gives "no retroactive `onMutationError` callback": apps "must record their own domain-level status". **S2 is therefore the cross-row, cross-user causal closure plus a durable, synced rejection record.** That is a smaller claim, but it is the case the forms-plus-photos domain hits: an inspection rejected for a revoked site, whose findings rows a supervisor's device has already annotated. In the PL literature, **No-Op** (ECOOP 2025) turns the loser of a conflict into a side-effect-free op, with a proof of convergence and invariant preservation ([LIPIcs](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2025.4)) [F]. Brocco (2026) separates identity trust from content trust, so replicas can exclude a compromised peer's updates without breaking causal dependents ([arXiv 2606.31759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31759)) [F]. Both neutralize single operations under a coordination-free merge. Neither compensates the dependents of a write that a trusted authority vetoes after devices applied it.

Every other product checked treats rejection per write. PowerSync tells backends to return 2xx even on validation failure, after which the client "will revert to the last known state". Its docs say nothing about dependent writes or attachments ([PowerSync](https://docs.powersync.com/handling-writes/handling-write-validation-errors.md)) [F]. Zero skips a mutation whose server mutator throws, reverts it on the client, and "the next mutation runs as normal" ([Zero](https://zero.rocicorp.dev/docs/mutators)) [F]. Realm reverted only the offending changeset through compensating writes ([MongoDB](https://mongodb.com/docs/atlas/app-services/sync/error-handling/errors)) [F]. The best evidence for S2 is Couchbase Lite. Forum reports from the 1.x era show **attachments uploaded even though the document push failed with 403**, and no API to detect the rejected doc ([Couchbase forum](https://www.couchbase.com/forums/t/403-missing-role-error/8018)) [F]. Current 3.x behavior was not checked. PowerSync's attachment helpers are labeled **alpha** in every SDK. They garbage-collect by *local* reference set plus an LRU archive, and say nothing about the order of upload versus row push or about a rejected row whose file is already uploaded ([PowerSync attachments](https://docs.powersync.com/client-sdks/advanced/attachments.md)) [F]. One note in the folder calls the JS helper GA, citing the Open Edition blog ([PowerSync](https://powersync.com/blog/powersync-open-edition-release)). The fetched docs page says alpha, and this report goes with the docs page. Academic referential-integrity work (I-confluence for foreign keys, IPA's "touch" repairs, Shapiro et al.'s causal-RI argument) covers rows under concurrent operations ([IPA](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08474); [Bailis](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf); [Shapiro et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03482)) [F]. It assumes every operation is eventually accepted. None of it models a trusted server vetoing a write after a device has already applied it locally, with side effects outside the log (bytes, claims, capabilities) that a state merge cannot undo. On the GC side, **Delta Lake** (PVLDB 2020) is the academic analog to cite for C2/S1/S5. Its transaction log in the object store decides which immutable files belong to a table, and vacuum removes unreferenced files after a retention window ([PVLDB](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p3411-armbrust.pdf)) [F; retention detail M]. Jazz applies the same shape to a local-first client, but keeps every version. What S5 adds to both is a retention window that does not reclaim a version still serving as the base of an offline device's pending writes.

### Folder sync: hand-built examples exist, a specified engine does not (now the strongest claim)

Two-way file ↔ DB sync exists in narrow, hand-built forms, always with one side canonical or with manual whole-record conflict picks. Lightroom flags "metadata changed externally" and makes the user choose ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/si/lightroom/help/advanced-metadata-actions.html)). An August 2026 bug report shows it **detecting conflicts and rewriting XMP when no edits were made**, a live failure of both F2 and F5 ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/bug-reports-674/p-lightroom-classic-repeatedly-detects-metadata-conflicts-and-writes-xmp-changes-when-no-edits-were-made-1635848)) [F]. darktable offers only reload-or-overwrite ([darktable](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.0/en/preferences-settings/storage/)) [F]. Joplin tells users not to edit sync-target files at all ([Joplin forum](https://discourse.joplinapp.org/t/joplin-does-not-show-latest-markdown-file-after-sync/23211)) [F]. The live threat is **Logseq**. Its DB version shipped a one-way Markdown Mirror with stable block IDs in May 2026, and two-way editing "is in active development on a feature branch", governed by an ADR 0017 that was not found publicly ([Logseq forum](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/whats-new-with-logseq-db-may-16th-2026/35020)) [F]. As of October 2026 it was still one-way ([listlis](https://listlis.com/blog/logseq-og-vs-database/)) [F]. Even if Logseq ships, it is one app, not an SDK. Watch it monthly anyway. On theory: TxForest proves serializable filestore transactions and load/store round-trip laws, but it covers neither crashes nor distribution ([arXiv 1908.10273](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1908.10273)) [F]. Dejima composes bidirectional transformations DB-to-DB only ([arXiv 2112.00288](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.00288)) [F]. No paper found combines lenses, multi-replica row sync, crash recovery and echo freedom, so F4 and F5 are the unclaimed laws.

The sweep made this the strongest claim, mostly by what it failed to find. Jazz 2.0's SPEC mentions a filesystem only as a chunk-store backend. It has no folder, watcher or file-replica concept, though its relational lenses carry a commutation law (INV-LENS-14) and bijective transforms (INV-LENS-17) that the thesis must cite beside Boomerang and Cambria ([10_lenses_migrations](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/10_lenses_migrations.md)) [F]. The file-synchronizer verification line stops in 2019. Its last entries are the Event-B-verified replicated file system of Najafzadeh, Shapiro and Eugster ([VMCAI 2018](https://www.lip6.fr/Marc.Shapiro/papers/2018/VMCAI-2018-filesys.pdf)) [F] and Syncpal ([DAIS 2019](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-22496-7_1)) [F]. **ViewBox** (FAST 2014) is the direct precedent for F4: Dropbox-style sync propagated local corruption and crash inconsistency to the cloud until uploads were restricted to crash-consistent views ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast14/technical-sessions/presentation/zhang)) [F]. The only 2024–2026 bidirectional-transformation sync paper found, Bifrons, is DB-to-DB ([arXiv 2502.18954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18954)) [F]. In industry, Tonsky's "Local, first, forever" runs the opposite direction, using a file-sync service as transport for a database ([tonsky.me](https://tonsky.me/blog/crdt-filesync/)) [F].

Jazz 2.0 joins the R+B+V cluster and adds partial E, so the gap that is left is F, plus E *integrated with* V. The two three-score clusters are **complementary halves of the proposal**. Today an app would need PowerSync or Zero, plus a Syncthing-like folder layer, plus its own E2EE layer, and it would hand-write the glue the proposal makes first-class. That is the honest product pitch.

In-house engines are the real benchmark for "has this been done". None of them is an SDK, and each solves one domain. Notion batches operations into transactions that the server validates against before/after state, fans out version numbers through MessageStore followed by a pull, shards 480 logical shards by workspace, and for offline mode (December 2025) moved offline pages to a CRDT model, tracked by an `offline_action` table of reasons ([data model](https://www.notion.com/blog/data-model-behind-notion); [offline](https://www.notion.com/blog/how-we-made-notion-available-offline)) [F]. Read as positioning, the proposal is **Linear's log + Figma's per-property merge + Dropbox's merge base + SPORC's validator placement + A's blob plane**, packaged as middleware over BYO Postgres.

Several parties could close the gap faster than a thesis can. **PowerSync** is the most dangerous: it already has R+V, a blob helper, and enterprise customers through the MongoDB Realm migration ([MongoDB](https://mongodb.com/blog/post/mongodb-atlas-power-sync-future-offline-first-enterprise-apps)) [F]. Adding E2EE or a stable attachment contract would erode C2/C3 as a product claim, though not the invariants. **Encrypted Spaces** and **NextGraph** (an "E2EE sync engine, SDK, graph DB, reactive ORM" at FOSDEM 2026 ([FOSDEM](https://archive.fosdem.org/2026/schedule/track/local-first/)) [F]) threaten the trust-mode story. **Logseq** threatens C4 for Markdown specifically. **Jazz 2.0** has moved furthest. Its spec already covers R+B+V, which pre-empts most of S1, S2 and S5 (see the verdict table). It is alpha, but the thesis must cite it as the closest system and state the deltas. The defensible moat is not features. It is the *specified and tested semantics* at the joints between rows, blobs and folders, which none of these teams publishes.

The money is real. Convex raised $57M in August 2026 [F]. Analyst sizing of "edge data sync" ranges from $2.36B ([TBRC](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/edge-data-sync-platform-global-market-report)) to $4.8B ([Market Intelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/edge-data-sync-platform-market/amp)) for 2025, at about 21–22% CAGR [F]. A twofold spread between analysts means the numbers are not reliable enough to cite as evidence of anything except interest. The documented use cases share one shape: **a relational record (form or job) plus N photos or scans captured offline, plus server validation and audit**. SafetyCulture's iAuditor, used by more than 9,000 organizations, was rebuilt on Couchbase Lite ([case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/couchbase/case-study/safetyculture-customer-case-study)) [F]. That shape, rows plus blobs under rejection, is exactly C3. **It is the strongest impact argument the thesis has**, stronger than folders or E2EE.

The adversarial reading is equally clear. The sync products that died owned the whole data model on a vendor backend. Standalone sync-SDK revenue is thin, so a skkil "enterprise product" should be priced as a component of skkil's own apps first. ## Extra domains fit as plugins around a small core, except live text and P2P

The design extends well because every mature system in every domain makes the same split: a **mutable, mergeable metadata layer** over **immutable, content-addressed bytes**. The rule for scope is that the core owns only what every domain needs and what the invariants depend on. Everything domain-shaped is a type plugin with a fixed hook set: codec, diff/merge (returning merged, conflict or unmergeable), chunker, derivation, scope with closure, lock policy, retention, and fetch mode.

| Concern | Core | Plugin | Out of scope (thesis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordering and integrity | Per-user server order, idempotent mutation table, **hash-chained log** | — | Byzantine fork *recovery* |
| Anti-entropy | `server_version` cursors for rows | **RBSR (Negentropy-style) over the blob hash set** for repair after restore or GC | Merkle tries à la Actual Budget (they solve a missing total order you don't have) |
| Versioning and branching | Snapshot + log replay to seq N (time travel); history retention window | Branch = `{base_seq, overlay log}`, merged by re-running named mutations; diff UI per app | Prolly/MST table indexes; Dolt-style cell merge of arbitrary snapshots |
| Blobs | CAS, leases/claims/fences, S1–S5 | Chunked resumable upload (tus/S3 multipart), range fetch, BLAKE3/Bao verified streaming, derivation recipes | Real-time A/V; online-only streaming filesystems |
| Collaboration | Opaque CRDT field type stored as snapshot + update rows | Actor-per-doc live mode, client coalescing, ephemeral presence, projection of title/mentions into rows | Building the live tier itself |
| Geo | Geometry as an atomic field (LWW or manual) | WKB codec + validity; bbox/polygon scope **with closure** and move-out tombstones; tiles as region-pack blobs | Vertex-level geometry merge |
| Locks | — | Lease/borrow policy for unmergeable data (Revit, Resolve, Git LFS lockable) | Merging opaque project files |
| Streams | — | Append-only type: device+seq IDs, TTL, downsample, priority export | Telemetry routed through the per-field merge log |

**Merkle structures and versioning.** A server-ordered log already has a total order, so a seq cursor is the optimal anti-entropy for rows. Merkle structures earn their place in three narrower roles. The first is the **hash chain** that makes S6 meaningful under E2EE. SPORC uses it for fork* detection ([SPORC](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall15/cos518/papers/sporc.pdf)) [F], at a cost of 32 bytes and one hash per op. The second is **RBSR over the unordered blob set**: Meyer's range-based set reconciliation costs O(d log n) and needs no shared history ([arXiv 2212.13567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13567v1)), and Negentropy implements it as Nostr NIP-77 ([GitHub](https://github.com/hoytech/negentropy)) [F]. The third is a per-scope state digest (LtHash as in WhatsApp [M], or an MST root) to catch silent divergence. Avoid Automerge-style Bloom sync on untrusted links, because adversarial hashes force O(#commits) round trips ([automerge-poison](https://github.com/hoytech/automerge-poison)) [F]. For history and branching, the closest products teach the cheap path. Neon's time travel is a base image plus WAL replay to an LSN within a retention window ([Neon](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/architecture-overview.md)) [F]. Your `server_version` is the LSN. Ink & Switch's Patchwork found that cheap, unnamed branches that fork only from main and are deleted on merge work well. It also found that **diff presentation needed per-domain work** ([Patchwork](https://www.inkandswitch.com/patchwork/notebook/2024-version-control/)) [F]. So history and branch mechanics go in the engine and diff UI stays in the app. Dolt shows what full Merkle-structured versioning costs: cell-level three-way merge and conflicts stored per primary key, but it owns the storage engine ([DoltHub](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2024-06-19-threeway-merge/)) [F]. Defer that. Two warnings carry over. A content-addressed system needs explicit deletion semantics, because atproto keeps no tombstones and CAR imports can resurrect deleted records [F]. And an E2EE server cannot compact ciphertext, an open problem in Automerge's Sedimentree design ([sedimentree.md](https://github.com/automerge/beelay/blob/main/docs/sedimentree.md)) [F], so clients must upload encrypted snapshots bound to `(seq, h_seq)`. Postgres 18 adds valid-time temporal constraints but still has no system versioning ([Neon](https://neon.com/postgresql/postgresql-18/temporal-constraints)) [F], so system-time history has to come from your log.

**High-frequency collaboration (the Notion case).** Keystroke-rate edits (5–10 per second per typist) must never each become a Postgres commit plus a global-log entry. Every production system puts **one authoritative in-memory copy per document** somewhere: a Figma process, a tldraw room in a Durable Object, Hocuspocus, Liveblocks ([tldraw](https://tldraw.dev/docs/sync); [Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-figmas-multiplayer-technology-works/)) [F]. Postgres serves as the checkpoint, not the hot path. Notion's data lake post reports that **90% of upserts are updates** across more than 200B blocks ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/building-and-scaling-notions-data-lake)) [F], so write amplification matters even in record mode. The open-source engines bolt Yjs onto row sync at demo level: PowerSync stores one row per Yjs update, uses a "very basic" merge function and works around BYTEA with base64 ([PowerSync](https://powersync.com/blog/postgres-and-yjs-crdt-collaborative-text-editing-using-powersync.md)), and Electric streams a `ydoc_update` shape ([y-electric](https://npmjs.com/package/@electric-sql/y-electric)) [F]. The right extension looks like this. Block structure stays in record mode, with parent and fractional index as one atomic field and a server cycle check, which is the Figma/Notion split. Rich text becomes a CRDT field whose live mode routes by docId to an actor, coalesces updates every 50–200 ms, keeps presence ephemeral, compacts updates into snapshots keyed by heads the Automerge way ([Automerge storage](https://automerge.org/docs/reference/under-the-hood/storage/)) [F], and writes only a coarse "doc advanced to heads h" mutation plus projected columns into the main log. That bridge invariant, consistency between a row's scalar fields and its doc heads, is a testable future contribution. It is not a four-month one.

**Geographic data.** Vector GIS fits best, and **geodiff is prior art you must cite**. It already does SQLite/GeoPackage ↔ PostGIS changesets with rebase and a conflict file ([geodiff](https://github.com/MerginMaps/geodiff)) [F]. QFieldCloud defaults to timestamp last-wins, with a manual "Re-apply" option ([QField](https://docs.qfield.org/it/reference/qfieldcloud/jobs/)) [F]. Esri's default branch version has no conflict detection at all ([Esri](https://doc.esri.com/en/arcgis-pro/latest/help/data/geodatabases/overview/branch-version-scenarios.html)) [F]. Your differences would be a server-ordered log, per-field rules and a generic schema. Nobody merges geometry vertices automatically, so geometry is an atomic field. The hard SDK problem is **spatial scope with referential closure**. OSM's bbox read returns ways with at least one node inside the box and relations non-recursively ([OSM API v0.6](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/API_v0.6)) [F], which is exactly how naive scoping breaks references. Tiles are derived, read-only bulk data, such as PMTiles extracts by bbox ([Protomaps](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/)) [F], and belong in the blob layer as region packs.

**Video, audio and other binaries.** Media never merges. Every pro tool uses proxies plus relink, pessimistic bin and timeline locks (DaVinci Resolve ([Blackmagic](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/uk/products/davinciresolve/collaboration)) [F]), or block streaming with a cache (LucidLink, which requires connectivity [F]). The blob plane therefore needs a resumable chunked upload protocol (tus, with 409 on offset mismatch ([tus](https://tus.io/protocols/resumable-upload)) [F]), "has-chunks" negotiation, range fetch, pin and LRU eviction, and derivations keyed by `(source hash, recipe, version)`. For E2EE media, follow Proton Drive's pattern: blocks of at most 4 MB encrypted independently, with a signed manifest of block hashes ([Proton](https://proton.me/blog/protondrive-security)) [F]. Accept that per-file keys rule out cross-user dedup. Elsewhere: CAD/BIM uses immutable object graphs (Speckle, content-hash IDs ([Speckle](https://docs.speckle.systems/developers/data-schema/concepts)) [F]), per-tab merges with some tabs unmergeable (Onshape ([Onshape](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Document/merging.htm)) [F]), or element borrowing (Revit [F]). Each maps to a merge plugin that can return "unmergeable" or to a lease plugin. Time-series and IoT data is an append-only stream type with TTL and priority export, like Greengrass stream manager ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/greengrass/latest/developerguide/stream-export-configurations.html)) [F], and should bypass the merge log. ML datasets need CAS snapshots with branch pointers (lakeFS/DVC [F]), not row sync. **Poor fits** to state explicitly as non-goals: real-time A/V, online-only streaming filesystems, serverless P2P mesh (a server-ordered log assumes the server is reachable, which is Ditto's turf), and opaque proprietary project files (.rvt, .als, .psd), where only locks and whole-file versions are possible.

## The server is distributed: one ordered source per scope, everything else disposable

Everything above assumed one logical server. The real deployment is multi-instance, uses Redis and in-memory caches beside Postgres, and is split into microservices, the way A's §8, §9 and §25 already are. The design survives this under one condition. **Three things must commit in one Postgres transaction on one shard: the scope's version counter, the mutation's effects, and the idempotency record.** Every other component, including caches, pub/sub, Kafka, CDC consumers, view servers and other services, is *downstream*. It may be stale, lose messages or deliver duplicates, because a client can always recover by pulling from the log by cursor. Every production system checked has this shape: one ordered source per scope, plus disposable readers.

| System | The single ordered source | Scaled-out, disposable part |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | One replication-manager tailing Postgres ([Zero](https://zero.rocicorp.dev/docs/self-host)) | Many view-syncers, each holding a SQLite replica |
| PowerSync | One replication process per source DB ([PowerSync](https://docs.powersync.com/maintenance-ops/self-hosting/multiple-instances)) | Stateless API containers |
| Electric | One holder of the replication lock ([Electric](https://electric.ax/docs/guides/deployment)) | HTTP shape log behind a CDN |
| Figma | One process per file; journal writes are conditional on holding the file's lock ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/making-multiplayer-more-reliable/)) | Routing layer, checkpoint storage |
| Cloudflare Durable Objects / tldraw | One actor per document ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/glossary/)) | Edge gateways |
| Notion | Postgres, 480 logical shards keyed by workspace on 32 physical hosts ([Notion](https://notion.com/blog/sharding-postgres-at-notion)) | MessageStore version pushes, caches |
| Linear | One global `lastSyncId` ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/scaling-the-linear-sync-engine)) | Delta fan-out to clients |

None of them uses Redis to decide order.

**Design rules for the SDK's server side.** These come from the research notes, with sources there.

1. **Sequencer.** Take `server_version` from `UPDATE sync_scope SET head = head + 1, hash = … RETURNING` inside the mutation's transaction, as its last lock. That single row is the sequencer, the fence and the hash-chain tip together. Do **not** use a `bigserial` column, Redis `INCR` or a Kafka offset. Database sequences commit out of order, so a cursor-based pull silently skips rows; Couchbase Sync Gateway users report abandoned-sequence problems of exactly this kind ([Couchbase forum](https://www.couchbase.com/forums/t/sync-gateway-assigns-abandoned-sequence-number/20231)). A *scope* is one user for personal data, or one workspace once collaboration arrives. Throughput is then bounded per scope, not globally. For a single user that bound is not a concern, but it needs a pgbench measurement for busy shared workspaces (unverified).
2. **Stateless instances.** Push and pull handlers hold no authoritative state, so any instance can serve any request, and routing by scope is only a cache-locality hint. If you later keep authoritative state in memory (an actor per document, as in the live-collaboration mode), its writes need fencing epochs, the way Figma conditions journal writes on a lock. This is the same idea as A's store-side fence.
3. **Idempotency in the database, not Redis.** Keep `last_mutation_id` per client in the same transaction, and advance it even when a mutation is rejected. That is Replicache's rule ([Replicache](https://doc.replicache.dev/reference/server-push)). An idempotency key stored in Redis can be lost on failover, and the mutation then applies twice.
4. **Cursors that survive topology changes.** Use `(epoch, scope, version, hash)`, derived from the data rather than from an instance. PowerSync's operation IDs are instance-local, so moving a user forces a full resync. When the cursor doesn't match, the server tells the client to reset and re-push its pending mutations ([Replicache reset](https://doc.replicache.dev/strategies/reset)).
5. **Cross-scope consumers read in commit order.** CDC, the outbox relay, search indexers and the media service read either by WAL LSN (Debezium) or by an `xid8` + `pg_snapshot_xmin` watermark, never by `id > last_id` ([outbox ordering](https://event-driven.io/en/ordering_in_postgres_outbox/)).
6. **Caches hold only immutable things.** Safe to cache: log entries by version, snapshots *at* a version, and "head is at least N" hints. **Not safe:** cache-aside of current rows on the pull path. A stale row paired with a new cursor means the client never re-reads it, which is a permanent divergence and breaks S4. If a mutable cache is unavoidable (permission lookups, hot settings), use version-guarded writes or memcache-style leases ([Facebook memcache, NSDI'13](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/nsdi13/nsdi13-final170_update.pdf)), invalidated from the commit log rather than by the writer.
7. **Fan-out is poke-then-pull over a lossy bus.** Redis pub/sub or NATS only tells clients "scope X is now at version N". Clients then pull from the log. Because the bus may drop messages, clients must also pull on reconnect, on a timer, and whenever they see a version gap. Kafka is for service-to-service events, not client notification.
8. **Read-your-writes across instances.** A push returns its version, and the next pull sends `min_version`. A read replica serves the pull only once its replay LSN has passed that version; otherwise the request goes to the primary.
9. **Microservices: a modular monolith per shard, with the rest event-driven.** The sync core (log, validator, scope rows) lives in one service and one database per shard. Business data that sync covers lives in the same database as the log. A sync log that has to be reconstructed from several databases is a distributed transaction in disguise. The **media service stays a separate service**, connected as follows:
   - **Outward:** sync-core outbox → media service. Each committed reference becomes an idempotent claim guarded by `seq`; this is A's §18, with the claim's `seq` taken from the sync log's version.
   - **Inward:** media events (`committed`, `deleted{reason}`) enter through an *inbox* that turns them into ordinary server mutations in the log.

   Clients therefore see **pending → committed → revoked** reference states instead of a promise of atomicity across services. When a mutation is rejected, the cascade in **S2 extends across services as compensating claim releases**. Rejection becomes a saga whose steps are idempotent and ordered by the log, which is the dual-write problem A already solves, one level up.
10. **Sharding.** Use logical shards keyed by scope, many more than physical hosts, as Notion does. A shard moves in four steps: copy, catch up on the log, fence with an epoch bump, then flip routing. Because cursors carry `(epoch, scope, version, hash)`, clients either continue seamlessly or are told to reset.
11. **Failover and restore.** Async replica promotion or a point-in-time restore can rewind the log. Either one must bump the epoch, and the hash chain then shows the fork: a client holding `h_n` that the server no longer has detects the rewind instead of silently diverging. Synchronous replication avoids rewinds that clients can see. Backups must restore the log and the media claims to a consistent cut, or S1 breaks.
12. **Test the distributed parts.** Jepsen found write loss and aborted reads in a Kafka-compatible transaction protocol ([Bufstream 0.1.0](https://jepsen.io/analyses/bufstream-0.1.0), Nov 2024), and G2-item anomalies under SERIALIZABLE in PostgreSQL 12.3 ([Jepsen](https://jepsen.io/analyses/postgresql-12.3), 2020). Hence two choices: Kafka stays downstream of the log, and ordering relies on the explicit row lock rather than on serializable isolation. Extend the deterministic simulator with multiple server instances, cache staleness, poke loss, replica lag, failover with a rewind, and a shard move. Add Elle-style log checks: versions dense per scope, monotonic per client, and no cursor ever skipping a committed version.

**What this adds to the thesis.**

- **Invariant D1** (distribution transparency): S1–S6 hold under any number of stateless instances, a lossy notification bus, stale immutable caches, replica lag, and shard moves or failover that bump the epoch.
- **Ablations:**
  - sequencer: `bigserial` vs. scope-row counter vs. WAL LSN;
  - pull path: cache-aside vs. pull from the log;
  - notifications: poke loss with vs. without periodic pull;
  - idempotency: Redis-held vs. database-held keys.

  Each ablation should produce measurable S4 or S1 violations in the simulator. That turns the architecture rules into evidence rather than assertion.

The idea is not novel: every rule above has a citation. Jazz 2.0's sharding chapter sketches the same shape (HLC positions, single-shard serializable transactions by default, a retained global sequencer), but marks it "exploratory" and specifies no implemented behavior (INV-SHARD-1) ([15_sharding](https://github.com/garden-co/jazz/blob/main/crates/jazz/SPEC/15_sharding.md)) [F]. A specified and simulation-tested D1 is therefore still open, though thin as a standalone claim. It is necessary, though, because a reviewer from industry will ask exactly this question first. In four months, implement rules 1–8 for a single shard and simulate rules 10–11. Leave real multi-region deployment to future work.

## Recommendation: lead with the folder replica and the trust-mode switch

**Framing.** Title it along the lines of *"Specified sync semantics for offline-first relational records that reference files: folder replicas, switchable trust and rejection"*. The thesis question becomes *"what must hold at the joints between rows, blobs and user files when offline writes are later rejected or the trust mode changes, and can one engine enforce it across domains?"*, not *"we built a cross-domain sync SDK"*. Present C1, C5 and C7 openly as reuse, citing Bayou, Replicache/Zero, Linear, Figma and Jazz 2.0. **Correction to the earlier ranking:** this section previously put S2 and S5 first. After the Jazz 2.0 SPEC, claim the contributions in this order. First, **F4/F5** on the folder replica. Second, **S6 as a runtime trust-mode switch**, carried by the per-user hash chain and tested under D1, positioned explicitly in the SPORC/Encrypted Spaces/Acumen lineage. Third, the **narrowed S2/S5/S1** deltas against Jazz: cross-row and cross-user causal closure with a durable rejection record, GC under a retention window that honors long-offline bases, and a cross-device pending → committed → revoked state. S1 remains the oracle the rest are checked against. Do not write "first two-way file ↔ DB sync", "no one has this validator design" or "first rejection cascade". All three are falsifiable today.

**Scope for about four months** (November to March, with December lost to finals per A's plan). Month one: finish A's core (I1–I6, the B2 vs P1 comparison) as chapter one. In parallel, build the minimal per-user server log with idempotent mutations, deferred FKs and the 2xx rejection protocol, with a hash chain added from day one. It is cheap and keeps S6 honest later. Run it from the start as two or more stateless instances behind the scope-row sequencer, with Redis used only for poke fan-out (distributed-server rules 1–8), so that D1 is tested from the beginning rather than bolted on later. Month two, the lead claim: one opaque mount (screenshots for `clip`) and one structured codec (`markdown_tasklist` for `time`) with F1–F5 as oracles, including crash-point injection for F4 and a quiescence check for F5, plus the seeded deterministic simulator they run in. Month three: S6 cross-mode equivalence, comparing a server-run validator against N clients replaying the same chained prefix, with a cut-point migration between modes. In the same month, the CAS layer with the pending → committed → revoked state, the narrowed S2 compensator (cross-row and cross-user dependents, durable rejection record) and S5 retention that honors offline bases, with per-mechanism ablations (claims, leases, fence). Month four: evaluation and write-up. If month three slips, keep S6 and cut S5 first, since Jazz covers most of it. Cut CRDT live mode, geo and media plugins, branching and time travel, S3 bounded revocation, key management and the declarative DSL to a future-work chapter that uses the plugin table above as its roadmap. Do not build a TLA+ model unless the simulator is done. A small Quint model of reject/claim/lease is the one formal artifact worth adding.

**Demonstrating cross-domain impact credibly.** Three apps whose *workloads* differ beat ten apps that are all the same. `clip` exercises the blob path heavily and conflicts lightly: an I-confluent, append-mostly workload. `time` exercises structural invariants (cycles, recurrence, bounded counts, fractional order) and the structured codec. Add a third, small **forms-plus-photos inspection app** (one inspection row, N photos, a server rule that rejects inspections for revoked sites). That is the shape SafetyCulture, AEDES and PowerSync's field-service customers actually pay for, and it is where the narrowed S2 bites. Jazz-style in-row blobs already prevent orphan photos, so the scenario to test is a rejected inspection whose findings a supervisor's device has already annotated in other rows. If a third app is too much, add a GeoPackage-style geometry field and bbox scope to it rather than building a separate GIS app. Report four kinds of evidence. Correctness: violations per 10^6 seeded schedules for each design, against baselines of a PowerSync-attachments-style local queue, Zero-style "no offline writes", A's online-only flow and, where feasible, Jazz 2.0's base-only cascade. Cost: convergence time against offline duration, bytes per sync, p99 local write latency, and orphan-byte persistence. Expressiveness: lines of declaration against hand-written sync code for each app, which is a first measurement with no prior baseline. Reproducibility: publish the oracle suite as a reusable harness, since no benchmark exists for rejection, blob integrity or folder replicas. Hughes et al.'s QuickCheck model, which found data-losing behavior in Dropbox and Google Drive, is the precedent that this style of work is publishable ([ICST'16](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~bcpierce/papers/mysteriesofdropbox.pdf)) [F]. ER-π, which replays interleavings against apps built on replicated-data libraries and reproduced 12 known bugs, is the 2025 baseline the simulator must be compared with ([Middleware 2025](https://people.cs.vt.edu/tilevich/papers/middleware2025.pdf)) [F]. If time allows, running the oracles against PowerSync attachments or Couchbase Lite 3.x and finding a real orphan-blob bug would be the single most persuasive impact result.

**Checks before you submit, in priority order.** Logseq ADR 0017's merge model. The unread Jazz 2.0 chapters (`2_data_model_identity`, `5_reads_snapshots`, `6_queries`, `16_maintained_subscription_views`, `19_native_relays`), to confirm that no cross-row cascade, cross-scope relay or folder concept hides there. The full texts of Acumen and SignalCD, for whether a server orders and whether a plaintext mode exists. Current Couchbase Lite 3.x attachment behavior on 403. Ditto attachment GC timing. CloudKit asset GC. The full texts of SCUBA, AQL/AntidoteSQL cascade semantics and Indigo. A 2022–2026 sweep of BX workshops and arXiv for "E2EE sync engine" and "lenses + filesystem + synchronization". Every [M] cell in the competitor table. These are the places where a reviewer could produce a counterexample.

## Conclusion

This review moved the thesis's center of gravity twice. The first pass demoted the validator, because SPORC published its core in 2010, and promoted blob-rejection semantics to the headline. The literature sweep reversed that second move. Jazz 2.0 specifies blobs inside the row transaction, a base-following rejection cascade and lease-aware refcount GC, which leaves S2 and S5 as narrow deltas. The claims that survive best sit where Jazz and the security literature stop. The first is the folder replica, which no system specifies and no paper has verified since 2019. The second is the *switch* between trust modes, which Jazz lists as an open question and Acumen and SignalCD do not attempt. The hash chain is what makes that switch testable: it turns S6 from "agrees with whatever the server showed" into "agrees on a hash-identified prefix". The forms-plus-photos domain still carries the impact argument, but now through cross-row, cross-user rejection rather than orphan photos. The broader lesson is that a normative spec in a vendor repository can pre-empt an academic claim as completely as a paper, so the watch list must include specs, not only venues.

For the product, the market evidence argues for being a component, not a company. Every vendor that tried to own the data model died or was absorbed, while PowerSync survives by attaching to customers' databases. A skkil sync layer that first runs skkil's own apps, keeps data in the customer's Postgres and bucket, and is distinguished by published invariants rather than feature count is the version with a credible path. The type-plugin boundary is what lets it grow into GIS, media and collaboration later without reopening the core.
